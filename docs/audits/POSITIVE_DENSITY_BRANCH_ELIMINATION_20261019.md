# Uniform sparsity on the product domain and elimination of the positive-density branch

19 October 2026. Offline. **PROVED reduction with an explicit classical
dependency.** The target and P12 remain OPEN. The finite estimate below
uses only the existing mixed-window and finite-pattern proofs. Its
qualitative corollary additionally invokes the standard qualitative
four-term case of Szemerédi's theorem. That classical theorem is stated
explicitly and is not reproved here; no quantitative rate or global
quadratic inverse theorem is assumed.

## 1. What changes in the open problem

The exact general negation in `O2_QUANTIFIERS_CUBIC_BOUND_20261017.md`
had two possible eligible sequences: a tending to zero with bounded
`delta/a^(3/2)`, or density bounded away from zero with that ratio tending
to zero. The second branch could not be omitted from the quantifier
manipulation alone.

The new finite-pattern argument, combined with the classical qualitative
theorem, now rules out EVERY positive-density eligible sequence on T5,
regardless of its phase correlations. Consequently failure of
DIRECT-Q-GAIN is equivalent to just the first branch. This simplifies
the open structural problem but supplies no bound at `a~1/log p`.

## 2. Uniform finite estimate on T5

Fix `0<a0<=4/5`. Let `L=log p>1`, and use exactly the original
mixed-window setup, with `r=d+s>=1`, `w=|Omega|/p`, `a0<=a<=4/5`,
`tau=a^2/L`, `H=floor(tau wp/(48r))`, and

    r[S+log(48r)+log L]<=L/100, S=log(1/w),             (PD1)
    mu0=(tau w/(48r))^(d+2s), mu>=mu0.

Assume also `log L>=4log(1/a0)`. Then

    w>=p^(-1/100), r<=L/(100log L),
    mu0>=p^(-3/100),
    p^(-1/2)/mu<=p^(-47/100),
    1/(pwmu)<=p^(-96/100),
    1/(p mu)<=p^(-97/100).                            (PD2)

Proof: nonnegativity of S and log(48r) gives the first two bounds. Since
`d+2s<=2r`, the original baseline obeys

    log(1/mu0)
      <=2r[S+log(48r)+log L+2log(1/a0)]
      <=L/50+4r log(1/a0)<=3L/100.                   (PD3)

All subsequent bounds follow by multiplication. Also

    tau wp/r>=100a0^2(log L)p^(99/100)/L^2->infinity,
    s(2+L)/(w sqrt(p))
       <=L(L+2)p^(-49/100)/(100log L)->0.             (PD4)

These bounds are uniform in every host presentation satisfying PD1 and
every a in [a0,4/5]. Thus H>=1 eventually. The bound
`H<=p/(48L)` gives `2H<p` for L>1. In particular all the original M13
conditions hold uniformly on this fixed positive-density range; this
does not require restricting to hosts generated by one chosen iteration.

Now choose any fixed integer `4<=m<p` and a verified finite bound R<m
for AP-free subsets of `{0,...,m-1}`. Define

    rho=m(m-1)/(24L)+L(L+2)p^(-49/100)/(50log L),
    e=p^(-47/100), d0=p^(-96/100), f0=p^(-97/100).     (PD5)

For the m-slot boundary `b_m`, equation F2 of
`FINITE_PATTERN_TERMINAL_20261018.md` gives `b_m/w<=rho`. If `rho<=1/2`
and `e<=1/2`, then the same note's finite bounds imply

    T_m>=wmu/4,
    E1/T_m<=4m[rho+e(1+rho)+2d0],
    E2/T_m<=4 binom(m,2)[(1+e)rho+d0+e].              (PD6)

The first follows from `(w-b_m)(mu-p^-1/2)`; the other two follow
from F4, the nonnegativity of all resonant weights, and `U<=w`.
Insert PD6 into F7. Since a>=a0, R>=1, and a<=1,

    |(R-1)/2-(m-1)a|<=3(m-1)/2,
    (m-R)/(m-1)<=1.

Therefore the following deliberately generous finite estimate holds:

    a <= (R-1)/(m-1)
         +(32/a0)[rho+(1+rho)e+d0]+4f0.              (PD7)

For completeness, the two error contributions before rounding constants
sum to at most
`(16/a0)[rho+(1+rho)e]+(28/a0)d0`; the diagonal is at most `4f0`.
Thus PD7 follows without an unidentified asymptotic error. For fixed m
and a0, every displayed error tends to zero uniformly over the entire
T5 class at density a>=a0.

This finite lemma does not use the classical theorem. For example,
`m=12,R=8` is supported by the complete elementary certificate already
in the repository. The restrictions on L, rho and H are explicit;
PD7 is not asserted for the small diagnostic primes.

There is also a simpler first-moment estimate, which needs no pair
near-positivity at all. Nonzero-difference occupancy K<=R, with the
diagonal restored, gives

    M1<=R T_m+(m-R)aw/p,
    (ma-R)T_m<=-S1+(m-R)aw/p.

The first-incidence bound in PD6 and a<=1 therefore imply

    a<=R/m+4[rho+e(1+rho)+2d0]+4f0.                 (PD7b)

Its terminal constant is weaker than PD7's, but it suffices for the
qualitative corollary below. In particular that corollary needs only the
original degree/erosion estimates and finite-pattern occupancy, not the
weighted pair theorem.

## 3. The explicit classical input and its corollary

The classical qualitative four-term theorem used here is precisely:

    For every eta>0 there exists an integer m such that every
    subset of {0,...,m-1} of size at least eta*m contains a
    four-term arithmetic progression of positive difference.
    Equivalently, r4(m)/m tends to zero as m tends to infinity. (PD8)

This standard theorem gives no rate sufficient for the research target.
We use only the existence of a fixed finite m depending on a0. Choose
m>=4 so that `r4(m)/m<a0/4`, and put R=r4(m). Take p sufficiently large,
depending on that m and a0, so that every hypothesis of PD7b holds and
its total error is less than a0/4. The result is `a<a0/2`, contradicting
a>=a0. One can also use the stronger PD7. Therefore:

    For every a0>0, there is p0(a0) such that no actual cyclically
    4-AP-free set on a T5 host has a0<=a<=4/5 when p>=p0(a0).
                                                               (PD9)

The supremum of the eligible relative densities thus tends to zero,
uniformly over all specified presentations of the mixed hosts. This is
a geometric transfer of the qualitative theorem; it is not a new proof
of that theorem and has no effective target-density rate stated here.
It is stronger than applying the full-group qualitative theorem directly:
the ambient density aw may vanish while relative density a stays fixed,
so the latter direct application would not suffice.

## 4. Simplified exact negation

Fix epsilon>0 and keep EVERY eligibility hypothesis of DIRECT-Q-GAIN,
including the logarithmic density lower bound. Put qrel=delta/a^(3/2).
Using PD9, the following are equivalent:

1. For this epsilon no constants c,p0 and function F>=1 diverging at zero
   give the proposed lower bound qrel>=cF(a) on all eligible p>=p0.
2. There is a sequence of eligible actual Boolean AP-free triples with
   `a_n->0` and qrel_n bounded above.                           (PD10)

One direction follows as before: the lower density bound forces
`p_n->infinity`, and bounded qrel cannot dominate cF(a_n).
For the converse, suppose no such bounded-ratio sequence exists. Define

    g(s)=inf{qrel: eligible triple with a<=s}, inf(empty)=infinity.

Then `g(s)->infinity` as s tends to zero; otherwise choose a sequence
with a_n<=1/n and bounded qrel_n. Choose 0<a0<=4/5 with g(a0)>=1, and use PD9
to choose p0 after which every eligible triple has a<a0. Define

    F(a)=min(a^(-1/4),g(a)) for 0<a<=a0,
    F(a)=1 for a>a0.

This function is finite, at least one, and divergent at zero. For any
eligible tail triple, `qrel>=g(a)>=F(a)`. Taking c=1 proves the desired
selection assertion for that epsilon, a contradiction. Empty lower
envelopes cause no problem because of the finite cap a^(-1/4).

Consequently the negation of the full assertion is now simply: for some
epsilon>0 there exists the sequence in item2. The positive-density
alternative in the October17 note remains a correct general logical
alternative, but is ruled out here by additional arithmetic input.

## 5. Remaining gap and verification scope

PD9 cannot be substituted for the desired rate. The unknown threshold
p0(a0) may grow too fast to take `a0=epsilon/log p`. Likewise PD10 gives
no bound on qrel along the still-possible a->0 sequence. The polynomial
density construction from October18 is ineligible and does not supply
that sequence. P12/W8, adequate phase selection, and the local target
therefore remain OPEN.
