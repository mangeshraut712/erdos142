## 1. Quantified simultaneous short shifts

Write p>3 prime, L=log p, n=|Omega|, w=n/p, and let d be the number of
recorded nonzero-character interval constraints of Omega. For every integer
Q>=2 there is a set H of at least ceil(p/Q^d) distinct shifts, including 0,
such that

    |h xi_i|_p <= floor(p/Q),  for every h in H and i,
    |Omega symmetric_difference (Omega+h)|/n <= 2d/(wQ).   (1)

Proof: partition [0,1)^d into Q^d half-open cubes. One cube contains at
least ceil(p/Q^d) of the p points ((x xi_i mod p)/p)_i. Fix one of those
points; subtract its index from all indices in the cube. They give distinct
shifts and include zero. Differences in each coordinate have magnitude
less than 1/Q, giving the first assertion. For a circular interval I,
|I\(I+delta)|<=|delta| for a centered residue delta. Multiplication by
xi_i is a bijection, and a union bound gives |Omega\(Omega+h)|<=dp/Q.
The two one-sided differences have equal size. This proves (1).

Consequently a relative error tau>0 is assured by

    Q=ceil(2d/(w tau)),
    |H| >= ceil(p/Q^d).                                   (2)

For a useful nonzero supply it suffices that d log Q<log p; if
d log Q<=L/2, at least p^(1/2) shifts are supplied. No claim that this
sufficient condition is necessary is made.

If functions |f_j|<=1 are supported on Omega, moving one argument by a
shift h changes its normalized integral by at most
|Omega symmetric_difference (Omega+h)|/n *only for its support indicator*.
An arbitrary f_j on Omega need not be approximately translation invariant;
small window boundary does not bound changes in A or B. In particular
(1) is geometry, not arithmetic coherence of the AP-free indicator.

### Worst H geometry versus geometry actually reached

H allows d as large as sqrt(L), w as small as exp(-sqrt(L)). At that
endpoint, the bound with the maximal Dirichlet scale Q~exp(L/d) gives
relative boundary at most order d, not o(1). Choosing Q to force even
fixed tau leads to

    d log Q = L + sqrt(L)[log(2d/tau)+o(1)] > L.             (3)

Thus the elementary short-shift argument does not guarantee a nonzero
tau-invariant shift uniformly over all H's allowed windows. Equation (3)
is a limitation of this bound, NOT an example proving there are no such
shifts. Large occupied coordinate windows may have additional structure
not captured by the union bound.

Along the proved conditional iteration the narrower bounds are

    d, log(1/w) = O_epsilon(sqrt(L)/log L + (log L)^2).

For any fixed m, tau=a^m and a>=epsilon/(2log n), equations (1)-(2) give

    d log Q = O_epsilon(L/(log L)^2 + sqrt(L)(log L)^C) = o(L)

for some fixed C. Hence p^(1-o(1)) simultaneous shifts have relative
window boundary at most a^m. This is a concrete supply at the reachable
geometries, but still says nothing by itself about changes of B.

## 2. Uniform quadratic invisibility on every H-window

Put C_p=2+log p. Let q(x)=c x^2+b x+c0 in Z_p with c!=0. Let F be
generated by s rotated quarter-character partitions, relative to Omega.
Then

    ||E_Omega[e_p(q)|F]||_2^2
       <= 4^s p^(-1/2) w^(-1) C_p^(d+s).                 (4)

This includes arbitrary rotations, arbitrary repeated/nonzero characters,
and arbitrarily small nonempty cells. It does not invoke full-group
orthogonality inside Omega.

### Fourier and Gauss proofs

A circular residue interval J has normalized Fourier l1 mass at most C_p.
Its zero coefficient is at most 1. For 1<=|k|<=p/2, the geometric series
gives

    |Jhat(k)| <= 1/[p |sin(pi k/p)|] <= 1/(2|k|).

Summing gives ||Jhat||_1<=1+H_floor(p/2)<=2+log p. Nonzero pullbacks
J(xi x) have the same l1 mass. Pointwise multiplication corresponds to
convolution of normalized Fourier coefficients, so the l1 mass is
submultiplicative. In particular a cell V of F has

    ||(1_Omega 1_V)hat||_1 <= C_p^(d+s).

For every frequency lambda, the normalized Gauss sum
E_x e_p(q(x)+lambda x) has magnitude exactly p^(-1/2). To see this,
square the unnormalized sum, substitute the difference h between the two
variables, and use sum_x e_p(2c h x)=0 unless h=0. The squared modulus
is p. Thus

    |E_p[e_p(q) 1_Omega 1_V]| <= p^(-1/2) C_p^(d+s).

Write mu_V=|Omega intersect V|/n and z_V=E_Omega[e_p(q)1_V]. Then
|z_V|<=mu_V and |z_V|<=p^(-1/2)w^(-1)C_p^(d+s). Therefore

    sum_V |z_V|^2/mu_V <=sum_V |z_V|
                        <=4^s p^(-1/2)w^(-1)C_p^(d+s),

proving (4). Zero-mass cells are omitted. This argument avoids every
small-cell denominator problem.

### Significance at the expanded linear-factor budget

Under H's geometry and s=O_epsilon(sqrt(L)/log L), the logarithm of the
right side of (4) is

    -L/2 + sqrt(L) + (d+s)log(2+L) + s log4
    = -L/2 + O_epsilon(sqrt(L)log L) = -L/2+o(L).           (5)

This s bound covers H's entire density range, not only a near its lower
endpoint. The function a^(-1/2)/log(2/a) decreases up to a=2e^(-2)
and then increases; its maximum on [epsilon/(2log n),4/5] is therefore
at an endpoint. Since log n~L uniformly here, the maximum is
O_epsilon(sqrt(L)/log L), including the ceiling and each fixed K.

Since ||e_p(q)||_(L2(Omega))=1, every F-measurable approximation v has

    ||e_p(q)-v||_2^2 >=1-4^s p^(-1/2)w^(-1)C_p^(d+s)
                    =1-o(1).                            (6)

Thus the expanded allowance does not make a genuine quadratic phase
approximable by linear quarter factors. This refutes that particular
generic approximation mechanism, including on H's relative windows.
It does not refute a mechanism that uses Boolean AP-exclusion to show
quadratic structure cannot be the only structure present.

## 3. Boolean quadratic interval hosts: all H assumptions except AP-free

Let S be a circular interval of residues, beta=|S|/p, and B=1_S(q(x)).
Its full-group Fourier l1 expansion in the variable q has mass <=C_p.
The constant term is beta. Apply the preceding Gauss estimate to every
nonconstant phase t q(x), t!=0. This gives

    |a-beta| <=p^(-1/2)w^(-1)C_p^(d+1),
    a=E_Omega B,                                         (7)
    Var_Omega(E_Omega[B|F])
       <=4^s p^(-1/2)w^(-1)C_p^(d+s+1).                  (8)

For (8), use |B-beta|<=1, the same cell argument as for (4), and subtract
the mean: variance of the projection is no larger than its squared norm.

At beta=epsilon/log p, (7) gives a=beta+exp(-L/2+o(L)), and (8) gives
o(a^2) uniformly over all admissible factors for each fixed K. Such hosts
have the desired density and uniform invisibility on every H-window.
They are NOT AP-free. For an initial interval window with n comparable
to p, beta can be chosen with fixed-factor slack so the lower density
threshold holds. For beta near epsilon/log p, it also holds on every
H-window for sufficiently large p since log n~log p.

The robust quadratic-host deletion theorem in
ROUTE_QUADRATIC_BOOLEAN_20261002.md rules out deleting o(|B|) points to
make the full-group host AP-free. Constant-fraction or more complicated
AP-free alterations are not excluded by that theorem; proving they force
linear-factor energy would require a new arithmetic argument.

## 4. Construction qualification checks

The symmetric base-16 window has rank r+1, n=4^r and p>100*16^(2r).
Already log(p/n) is a positive fixed fraction of log p, so it violates
n>=p exp(-sqrt(log p)). Taking still larger p only worsens that failure.
Even if r+1<=sqrt(log p) is achieved by padding p, the size condition fails.
Thus it is not an H counterexample.

For full-product Freiman shell images, the density obstruction in
ROUTE_CONSTRUCTIONS_20261002.md rules out a logarithmic cyclic density
with polylogarithmically many shells. This alone does not exclude a shell
as a subset of a larger *relative* window, but neither a compliant large
window nor uniform invisibility for an AP-free relative shell was obtained.

CRT and disjoint random-block attempts do not solve this: CRT prime
transfer is not automatic, and block replication preserves the original
seed density while creating additional cross-block progressions.

No actual construction in this run satisfies all of H, including AP-free
Booleanity and invisibility at the expanded character budget.

## 4A. Every H-window quadratic interval host has nonzero 4APs

There is also a relative counting theorem, so failure of AP-freeness of
the preceding hosts is verified on every H-window, not merely full Z_p.
Let W=1_Omega, B=1_S(q(x)), and put

    Z_S=sum_t |Shat(t)|^2 |Shat(3t)|^2.

Then

    |Lambda4(WB)-Z_S Lambda4(W)|
       <=p^(-1/2) ||Shat||_1^4 ||What||_1^4
       <=p^(-1/2) C_p^(4d+4).                             (9)

Proof: Fourier-expand S in its argument q and W in its argument x in
each of the four vertices. The quadratic part in (x,h) is

    c[(sum t_j)x^2+2(sum j t_j)xh+(sum j^2 t_j)h^2].

Its vanishing is exactly the three equations sum t_j=sum j t_j=
sum j^2 t_j=0, whose solutions are t_j=t(1,-3,3,-1). In this case the
entire polynomial sum_j t_j q(x+jh) vanishes, including its linear and
constant parts. The S-coefficient product is |Shat(t)|^2|Shat(3t)|^2,
so these terms give Z_S Lambda4(W).

All other t yield a nonzero quadratic in (x,h), even after adding the
linear terms contributed by W. Its normalized two-variable Gauss sum
has magnitude <=p^(-1/2): if the x^2 coefficient is nonzero, first sum
in x and bound the remaining sum trivially; if x^2=0 but xh!=0, the
x sum forces at most one h and the bound is 1/p; if only h^2 remains,
sum in h. Multiplying the four l1 masses of each expansion proves (9).

For a narrow coordinate window, the endpoint-label identity gives

    Lambda4(W)=p^(-2) sum_label |Omega_label|^2 >=w^2/3^d. (10)

Here labels are the coordinate integer lifts modulo 3. Equal labels
place both endpoint convex combinations in their defining intervals;
the no-wrap diameter condition and invertibility of 3 give the converse.
Thus (10) includes the diagonal and uses at most 3^d labels, as proved
in frontier_audit_geometry.md section 2.

For an interval S with 0<beta<=1/12, set T=floor(1/(6beta)). For
1<=|t|<=T the geometric-series formula and sin(pi u)>=2u on 0<=u<=1/2
give |Shat(t)|,|Shat(3t)|>=2beta/pi. Since T>=1/(12beta),

    Z_S >=(8/(3pi^4)) beta^3.                              (11)

At beta~epsilon/log p under H's geometry, the main term in (9) is at
least a constant times beta^3 exp[-(2+log3)sqrt(L)], while its error is
exp[-L/2+O(sqrt(L)log L)]. The main term also exceeds 1/p, the maximum
possible diagonal contribution to Lambda4(WB). Hence the host contains
a nonzero 4AP for all sufficiently large p, uniformly over H-windows.

This proves AP-richness but does not prove a constant-fraction deletion
bound on every H-window. A full-group completion upper bound combined
with (10) would only give a hole fraction of order w/3^d, which can tend
to zero at H's endpoint geometry. No stronger relative deletion bound is
asserted without additional work.

## 5. Scope of the remaining obstruction

Short-shift supply is abundant on the narrower geometries reached by the
conditional closure. But (6)-(8) show that short-shift geometry and low
linear-factor energy alone coexist with a globally coherent quadratic
explanation. An unconditional proof must exploit the precise exclusions
of the actual AP-free indicator, rather than approximate arbitrary
quadratic phases with the permitted linear characters. No such final
implication is established here.
