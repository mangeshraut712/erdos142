## 1. Chi-square form — PROVED

Refining F by P can only increase ||E(f_2|F)||. For F refined by P, so
that each cell V lies in one class c,

    ||E(f_2|F)||^2 <= max_c w_c(1-w_c) * E_n chi^2_n,
    chi^2_n = sum_{V in class(n)} (P(V|n) - P(V|class))^2 / P(V|class).

Proof: in SYMMETRIZATION section 1, apply Cauchy–Schwarz in n within each
class, then use E[f_2^2|c] = w_c(1-w_c), since f_2 is an indicator minus
its class mean.

So the energy is at most a(1+o(1)) times the chi-square dependence between
the count vector and the cell, given sigma mod 15. This quantity depends
only on the uniform measure. (The same step sharpens SYMMETRIZATION
section 2 to junta energy <= 3 a s/r (1+o(1)).) Checked exactly: r=6, 60
random symmetric A, factors of 1 to 3 random arcs; worst ratio 0.20.

## 2. Strongly generic factors — PROVED

Let F be generated by quarter arcs of phi_1,...,phi_k with k=O(log r),
refined by P, and put x_m=sum_j m_j phi_j and H=r. Suppose

    Psi(x_m) >= T := 2k log(4 log r) + C_0 log r   for all 0 != m in [-H,H]^k.

Call n typical if |n_d - r/4| <= sqrt(C_1 r k) for every d. Then
P(n) >= r^(-O(1)) for typical n, and the atypical mass is at most
r^(-2) 4^(-k).

For typical n, the decay lemma with |T|=1 gives
|E[e(x_m X)|n]| <= e^(-Psi/2)/P(n). Sandwich each cell (a product of k
arcs) between Beurling–Selberg majorants and minorants of degree H
(Cochrane's construction for products). Their nonzero Fourier
coefficients have total mass at most 4^(-k)(1+(8/pi)(1+log H))^k, and
their means are 4^(-k)(1+O(k/H)). With C_0 large enough this gives

    P(cell | n) = 4^(-k)(1+eps_n),   |eps_n| <= 8k/r   (typical n),

and the same for P(cell | class), averaging over n with the atypical
mass absorbed. Hence chi^2_n <= 2(16k/r)^2 for typical n. For atypical n,
chi^2_n <= 15*4^k, so their contribution is at most 15 r^(-2). Therefore

    ||E(f_2|F)||^2 <= a(1+o(1)) (512 k^2 + 15)/r^2 = o(a^2/log(2/a)).

The (log H)^k = r^(Theta(log log r)) coefficient mass that blocked earlier
attempts is absorbed by e^(-T/2), because T is of order log r log log r.

## 3. Mixed factors: absorption works, except for lattice coefficients — OPEN

Factors in which no combination is moderately structured are now covered:
- individually exceptional characters (CELL_BOOKKEEPING section 5 with
  SYMMETRIZATION section 3);
- strongly generic ones (section 2).

The remaining case is a factor in which some combinations x_m with
m in [-H,H]^k have Psi(x_m) < T.

**Absorption without a tower.** Let S' be the current junta, and say m is
relatively exceptional if Psi restricted to i not in S' is below T.
Repeat: pick such an m outside the rational span of those already chosen,
and add its bad indices and run tops (at most (T/c^2+1)(g+1) coordinates)
to S'. Each round raises the rank, so there are at most k rounds, and
|S'| <= k(T/c^2+1)(g+1) = polylog(r), well within the o(sqrt(r)/log^(5/2) r)
(PARAMETER_AUDIT section 3; the original o(sqrt(r)/log r) assumed a ~ r^(-1/2))
allowed by SYMMETRIZATION section 2. The threshold T stays fixed, which
avoids the tower of CELL_BOOKKEEPING section 6, item 3.

At the end, every m in [-H,H]^k outside the span Lambda_Q of the absorbed
vectors b_1,...,b_d satisfies Psi_out(x_m) >= T and is decayed on the
coordinates outside S' (section 2 applies conditionally on u_(S'), since
f_2 1_W is count-symmetric there).

**The obstacle.** Frequencies with m in Lambda_Q ∩ [-H,H]^k are rational
combinations sum q_l b_l, with denominators up to det and numerators up to
(kH)^k. Their phases are junta-determined only if sum |q_l| is small: the
drift of each b_l is r^(-M), multiplied by sum |q_l|. Minkowski reduction
bounds the coefficients by k^(O(k)) H^(O(1)) = r^(O(log log r)) in the
worst case. So the drift precision must be r^(-O(log r log log r)), which
needs g of order log^2 r log log r. That is still polylog, but the
undecided-set bound and the boundary regions of V∩W in the complementary
phases must then be controlled at the same scale. This is the one
remaining technical statement:

**Basis lemma (OPEN).** The saturation of Lambda_Q ∩ Z^k has a basis
completable to a unimodular matrix whose entries are at most r^(O(1))
(or r^(polylog)), uniformly over choices of phi_1,...,phi_k.

For example, pairs phi_(2i) = phi_(2i-1) + k_i/15 + y_i with sparse y_i
give the basis {e_(2i)-e_(2i-1)}, which has entries in {0,+-1}, so these
factors are proved by the absorption argument.
